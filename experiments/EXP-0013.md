# EXP-0013 — Concurrent GPU workload interference

**Date:** 2026-09-30
**Status:** COMPLETE — measurement hazard identified

## Question

Why did training throughput fluctuate between 3,600 and 295 tokens/s?

## Result

| Condition | Tokens/s |
|---|---:|
| Training alone | 3,300–3,700 |
| Training + background Wikipedia crawler | **295** |
| Training + compute benchmark | 1,666 |
| Training + 4B instantiation attempt | process terminated (access violation) |

## Cause

Two stale background processes (`crawl_wikipedia_categories.py`, launched hours earlier and
still running) plus foreground benchmarks were contending for the same GPU.

```
Get-Process python | Select-Object Id, CPU, WorkingSet64
Stop-Process -Id 3976, 16144 -Force
```

After killing them, throughput returned to 3,362 tok/s with no other change.

## Interpretation

**All throughput numbers in this repository must be measured with the GPU otherwise idle.**
A figure taken under contention is not a hardware property and should not be compared across
runs. The 2.80 TFLOP/s matmul peak in [EXP-0001](EXP-0001.md) was measured on an idle GPU;
the 1.50 TFLOP/s figure from a later benchmark was measured while training ran, and the
difference is contention, not regression.

This is also a real limitation for a single-GPU workstation: measurement and training cannot
share the device.

## Next action

Re-run `scripts/bench_compute.py` and `scripts/bench_inference.py` with the GPU idle, and
record the clean figures. Do not run benchmarks concurrently with training.
